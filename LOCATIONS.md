# Dublin Treasure Hunt — Locations, Clues & Hints

_The 45 landmark stops used across the ten team routes. Each team visits 5 of these before the secret final destination. Clues are what teams see; hints appear only when they tap **“Need a Hint?”**._

## Landmarks

| # | Location | Clue (shown to teams) | Hint |
|---|----------|-----------------------|------|
| 1 | 🌉 **Samuel Beckett Bridge** | Named for an Irishman who waited endlessly for Godot, I arc across the Liffey like a harp turned on its side. Cross me to leave the south bank behind. | The striking white cable-stayed bridge just north of Grand Canal Square. |
| 2 | ⛵ **Jeanie Johnston Tall Ship** | A three-masted replica of a famine ship, I never lost a single soul on my Atlantic crossings. I'm moored on the north quays waiting to be boarded. | A wooden tall ship on Custom House Quay, north bank. |
| 3 | 🚢 **EPIC / CHQ Building** | Once a bonded warehouse for tobacco and wine, my vaulted vaults now tell the story of ten million who left these shores. Find me right on the quay. | The CHQ building on Custom House Quay — home of EPIC The Irish Emigration Museum. |
| 4 | 🏛️ **The Custom House** | James Gandon gave me my neoclassical grandeur in 1791. Burned in 1921, I rose again — my green copper dome still watches over the river. | The grand domed building on the north bank, near Butt Bridge. |
| 5 | 🗿 **Famine Memorial** | We are gaunt bronze figures walking forever toward the emigrant ships. Our thin faces remember Ireland's darkest years. | On Custom House Quay, just east of the Custom House. |
| 6 | 🛢️ **Convention Centre Dublin** | A great glass barrel tilted on the north quays, lit up at night — Dubliners joke I look like a giant pint glass laid on its side. | On the north bank at Spencer Dock, near Samuel Beckett Bridge. |
| 7 | 🚢 **MV Cill Airne** | A 1960s riverboat, once a tender ferrying passengers out to ocean liners, now a floating bar and restaurant moored on the quay. | A moored vintage steamship on North Wall Quay. |
| 8 | 🛶 **Grand Canal Dock Basin** | Kayakers and paddleboarders skim across me now, but I was once crowded with cargo barges. The glass towers of the tech giants watch my still water. | The large water basin just south of the theatre. |
| 9 | ⚓ **The Diving Bell** | A giant iron chamber that once lowered workers to the riverbed to build Dublin's deep quay walls. Now I stand as a rusty-red monument on the boardwalk. | On Sir John Rogerson's Quay, south bank. |
| 10 | 🎸 **Windmill Lane** | A wall of ever-changing graffiti marks the lane where a very famous Dublin band recorded their earliest albums. Fans still leave their mark on me. | Windmill Lane, off Sir John Rogerson's Quay. |
| 11 | 🚪 **Grand Canal Sea Locks** | Great wooden gates where the canal steps down to meet the tide — the very last lock before boats slip out into the Liffey and the sea. | Where the Grand Canal joins the Liffey, at the east end of the dock. |
| 12 | 🏭 **Boland's Mill** | In Easter 1916 a young commandant named de Valera held these old flour mills against the British. My tall silos still stand by the dock. | Barrow Street, beside Grand Canal Dock. |
| 13 | 🏉 **Aviva Stadium** | A shimmering glass bowl rising over the rooftops of Lansdowne — I roar to life on match days for rugby and football. | Lansdowne Road — follow the river east then head south. |
| 14 | 🌁 **Ha'penny Bridge** | Dublin's most photographed crossing — an elegant white iron arch since 1816. Once you'd pay a half-penny to cross me; now I'm free. | The pedestrian bridge linking Temple Bar to Liffey Street. |
| 15 | 🎭 **Temple Bar Square** | Cobblestones, buskers and more pubs than you can count — Dublin's cultural quarter. My name honours a Sir Temple, not a place of prayer. | South of the Liffey, between Dame Street and the river. |
| 16 | 🏦 **Bank of Ireland, College Green** | Once the Irish Parliament — the first purpose-built two-chamber house in the world. I curve grandly opposite Trinity's gates. | The great columned building facing Trinity College on College Green. |
| 17 | 🐚 **Molly Malone Statue** | She wheeled her barrow through streets broad and narrow, crying 'cockles and mussels, alive alive oh!'. | On Suffolk Street, just off the bottom of Grafton Street. |
| 18 | 🎓 **Trinity College Front Gate** | Ireland's oldest university, founded by a queen in 1592. The Book of Kells sleeps behind my walls. Enter beneath my grand arch. | The main entrance on College Green. |
| 19 | 🌳 **Merrion Square** | A perfect Georgian square of red brick and rainbow-coloured doors. Poets and patriots once lived all around my leafy park. | The large Georgian square just east of Leinster House. |
| 20 | 🎭 **Oscar Wilde Statue** | I lounge on a boulder in my smoking jacket, wit dripping from my lips, watching over the square where I spent my childhood. | The reclining statue in the corner of Merrion Square park. |
| 21 | 🦌 **Natural History Museum** | Dubliners call me the 'Dead Zoo' — two crowded floors of stuffed beasts and glass cases, barely changed since Victorian times. | On Merrion Street, beside Leinster House. |
| 22 | 🖼️ **National Gallery of Ireland** | A long-lost Caravaggio masterpiece hangs within my walls, alongside the very best of Irish art — and it costs nothing to come in. | On Merrion Square West, beside the government buildings. |
| 23 | 🏛️ **Leinster House** | A Georgian ducal palace that now houses the Dáil — the seat of Ireland's parliament. A harp flies above my gates. | Between Kildare Street and Merrion Square. |
| 24 | 🏺 **National Museum (Archaeology)** | Bog bodies, Viking gold and the great Tara Brooch sleep beneath my domed rotunda. Ireland's ancient treasure lives in me. | On Kildare Street, beside Leinster House. |
| 25 | 🔑 **Fitzwilliam Square** | The last and smallest of the great Georgian squares — my central garden is private still, locked and open only to keyholders. | South of Merrion Square, off Fitzwilliam Street. |
| 26 | 🦢 **Wilton Park** | Tucked away from the Dublin bustle, this green escape has watched the city grow around it. Find the park where Georgian streets give way to open skies right by the canal. | Every city needs a breath of fresh air — find Dublin's little patch of green on Wilton Terrace. |
| 27 | 🕊️ **Mount Street Bridge** | Here in 1916 a handful of Volunteers held off hundreds of soldiers marching up from the sea. A fierce battle by this quiet water. | Where Northumberland Road crosses the Grand Canal. |
| 28 | 🦆 **St Stephen's Green** | Twenty-two acres of green in the city's heart. During the 1916 Rising both sides paused their fighting so the keeper could feed my ducks. | The large park at the top of Grafton Street. |
| 29 | 💒 **University Church (Newman)** | Behind a plain and narrow front on the Green I hide a jewel-box of Byzantine colour — a favourite little church for weddings. | On the south side of St Stephen's Green, beside Newman House. |
| 30 | 🩺 **Royal College of Surgeons** | My columns on the west side of the Green still carry the bullet scars of 1916, when the Citizen Army held me against the soldiers. | The grand building on the west side of St Stephen's Green. |
| 31 | 🏙️ **Little Museum of Dublin** | A Georgian townhouse crammed with the story of 20th-century Dublin, every object donated by the ordinary people of the city. | On the north side of St Stephen's Green. |
| 32 | 🏨 **The Shelbourne Hotel** | Dublin's grandest hotel since 1824 — the 1922 Constitution was drafted in my Room 112, and bronze princesses flank my front door. | On the north side of St Stephen's Green, facing the park. |
| 33 | 🎻 **National Concert Hall** | Once the great examination hall of a university, I am now Ireland's home of orchestral music, just south of the Green. | On Earlsfort Terrace, a short walk south of St Stephen's Green. |
| 34 | 🌿 **Iveagh Gardens** | Dublin's 'secret garden' — a hidden Victorian pleasure ground with a rosarium, a maze and a waterfall that few tourists ever find. | Tucked behind the National Concert Hall, entrance off Clonmel Street. |
| 35 | 🎶 **Grafton Street** | Dublin's most famous shopping street, paved just for strolling and alive with buskers — a 'wonderland' in a well-known song. | The pedestrian street between Trinity College and St Stephen's Green. |
| 36 | 🛍️ **Powerscourt Townhouse** | A grand Georgian merchant's mansion, now a hidden courtyard of cafés and boutiques beneath a soaring glass roof. | On South William Street, just off Grafton Street. |
| 37 | 🏬 **George's Street Arcade** | A red-brick Victorian market arcade, one of the oldest in Europe, full of quirky stalls, vinyl records and fortune-tellers. | Between South Great George's Street and Drury Street. |
| 38 | 🎩 **The Mansion House** | The Lord Mayor of Dublin has lived in my Queen Anne house since 1715, and the very first Dáil met in my Round Room in 1919. | On Dawson Street, between Trinity and St Stephen's Green. |
| 39 | 🏛️ **Freemasons' Hall** | The home of the Grand Lodge of Ireland — behind my doors are rooms decked out as Egyptian temples, Gothic chapels and more. | On Molesworth Street, off Dawson Street. |
| 40 | 🎸 **Phil Lynott Statue** | A bronze rocker with his bass guitar — the frontman of Thin Lizzy, standing outside the pub where the music crowd still gathers. | On Harry Street, off Grafton Street (outside Bruxelles). |
| 41 | 📚 **National Library of Ireland** | On Kildare Street I keep the nation's memory — first editions, ancient maps and the very notebooks of Yeats and Joyce sleep beneath my great domed reading room. | On Kildare Street, beside Leinster House and facing the National Museum. |
| 42 | 🚆 **Tara Street Station** | A slim red-brick clock tower marks Dublin's busiest little station on the river's edge — thousands rush through me each day to catch the DART. | On Tara Street, just south of the Liffey near Butt Bridge. |
| 43 | 🚉 **Pearse Station** | Behind my Victorian front on Westland Row hides a vast iron-and-glass train shed. I carry the name of two brothers who gave their lives in 1916. | The DART/rail station on Westland Row, just behind Trinity College. |
| 44 | 🍻 **The Pádraig Pearse** | A proper old Dublin pub on the long street that honours a patriot-poet — find me among the Georgian doors and student haunts of Pearse Street. | The pub called The Pádraig Pearse, at 139 Pearse Street. |
| 45 | 🏫 **The Schoolhouse** | Built in 1861 to teach the children of the parish, I traded chalk for pints long ago — now my bar and terrace sit by the canal in leafy Ballsbridge. | The Schoolhouse Hotel & Bar on Northumberland Road, near Mount Street Bridge. |

## 🤫 Secret finish — revealed only in each team's final clue

**O'Donoghue's** — 15 Merrion Row, Dublin 2, D02 PF50

**Final clue (cryptic — no address, no pub name):**

> No address this time — you'll have to earn the last one. 🎵
>
> In 1962, in a snug little bar just off the NORTHEAST corner of St Stephen's Green, a few ballad singers pulled up stools, started a session, and took their band's name from the very city all around them. From that back room came Ireland's most famous folk group — and to this day the fiddles, bodhráns and singalongs still spill out its door most nights.
>
> Follow the music to the little pub where 'The Dubliners' were born. It sits on the short row that runs off the top corner of the Green, in the shadow of the city's grandest hotel.
>
> Take a team photo OUTSIDE the door — then head in for the session and join everyone else!

**Hint (safety net):** It's O'Donoghue's — the legendary trad-music pub on Merrion Row, right beside the Shelbourne Hotel (and the tiny walled French graveyard next door).
